# Interview 01 - Labeling / regulatory practitioner (anonymised)

- Role: labeling or regulatory professional at a medical device organisation (name and employer withheld)
- Channel: written chat, conducted by Siddhant; 5 questions answered
- Date: 2026-10-15 (as recorded; confirm)
- Permission: answers are used anonymously for the case project
- Reliability: one practitioner, answers are estimates, not measurements

## What they said (close paraphrase)

| Topic | Answer |
|---|---|
| Steps from request to release | Change request initiated, input collection, change impact assessment, approval of the assessment, label redlining, label design update, proofreading, technical labeling review by stakeholders, approval of artwork, preparation for release, label release, implementation |
| Typical duration | Depends on the number of labels affected; about 3 months on average |
| Where it waits longest | Change impact assessment, and approvals from all stakeholders |
| How regulation changes are found | Manual. Information comes from regulatory teams and common groups; senior managers hold subscriptions that email them when something changes |
| Review rounds | Three at the designer end (self review, peer review, proofreader) plus a separate stakeholder review. Each review takes about 20 minutes per label on average, and volume drives the total |

## What it means for IntelliLabel AI

- Confirms the two bottlenecks we target: impact assessment (Impact engine, 18 of 18 labels in Scenario A) and approval waiting (risk-routed signing, release package).
- Confirms regulation tracking is manual and email-driven (Regulatory Watch page, `engines/reg_monitor.py`).
- Confirms repeated review levels (self, peer, proofreader) are the repeated checks that rules R01-R21 can take over.
- Their step list matches our pipeline: intake, impact, drafts, diff and compliance, approval, release, audit.

## Cautions

- 20 minutes per label is per review; the total per label depends on how many of the four review stages apply. Multiplying (up to about 80 minutes per label) is our inference, not their statement.
- No numbers were given for label counts, error rates or cost.
